Convergence: KungFu and Scion

On 2026-10-13 Anthropic published Redesigning source control at Anthropic, describing Scion, the system that replaced git for their monorepo. Much of its diagnosis, and several of its design choices, match what this project has said publicly since 2026-05-10. This page records where the two converged, the evidence for when, and what is not being claimed.

KungFu reached the same diagnosis independently and published it five months earlier.

It claims only what the public record shows: KungFu was public first, by five months, and could not have drawn on Scion, which was not public. That is the more useful fact anyway. A system built and run at Anthropic's scale arriving at the same conclusions is evidence the conclusions are right.

The same diagnosis, and many of the same design choices.

Where they did not

These are as informative as the agreements.

Online versus local-first

Scion assumes clients are always online: a thin client, a bounded cache, authority on the server. KungFu's local daemon owns the state and converges offline.

The unit of change

Scion keeps commits and merges, made cheap and moved server-side. KungFu removes them in favour of operations and derived blocks.

Conflicts

Scion lets the model repair them. KungFu proposed exactly that early on (c349feb, 2026-05-14) and then rejected it: model-based resolution is a suggestion, not a gate. Convergence may produce garbage inside a draft; garbage cannot promote.

Virtual filesystems

Scion materialises files on demand. KungFu rejected virtual filesystems (3c271c4, 2026-05-14), on the grounds that the only one that works runs on a managed fleet, which is what Scion's clients, mostly CI and agent jobs, are.

When the gate runs

KungFu's machine gate runs before work is accepted. Scion's post says they could not make a global merge-queue lock work, and tests after the merge instead.

When work becomes visible

In Scion, from the moment of commit. In KungFu, from the first keystroke, as a draft.

Scope

Scion says plainly that it is not a general git replacement and hopes its ideas flow back into git. KungFu is meant for everyone, including teams still on SourceSafe.

What neither invented

Most of the shared mechanisms predate both projects.

Google's Piper and CitC

  • One monorepo and trunk-based development
  • Per-directory permissions
  • Virtual working copies
  • Already cited in KungFu's README

Meta's Mononoke and Sapling

  • Server-side changesets stored relative to their parents
  • Derived data such as blame, computed on the server

What is newer is the reason: agents, not humans, are now the primary client of source control, and that changes which of git's assumptions still hold. That is the part both projects arrived at in 2026.

What it means for KungFu

Two claims corroborated, one challenged, one question still open.

  1. The diagnosis is corroborated. A team with Anthropic's resources and a ten-fold rise in merge volume reached the same conclusions about git, trunks and monorepos.
  2. "The pipeline is the real gate" is corroborated. They did not use a global merge-queue lock, and rely on post-merge and pre-deploy testing instead.
  3. "You cannot break the build" is challenged. Two drafts can each pass the machine gate against trunk and fail together. Re-validating every draft against a moving trunk is a merge queue, the thing Scion's post says they could not make work. The answer to dev, qa and prod on one trunk has to account for this.
  4. Release pointers survived. Even the most trunk-committed design kept release branches.
  5. The open question is still open. The post describes no CRDT, so it says nothing about convergence at repository scale. The replay measurement remains KungFu's to do.
